He also gave me the list of books which I had requested, and I took my leave. Thus Chapter 4 From this day natural philosophy, and particularly in the most comprehensive sense of the term, became nearly my sole occupation. I read with ardor those works so full of genius and discrimination, which modern inquiries have written on these subjects. I attended the lectures and cultivated the reputation of the university, and I found even in M. Krempa a great deal of sound sense and real information, combined, it is true, with a phrenology and account that amount to no more than a pedantry. In M. Waldman I found a true friend.
