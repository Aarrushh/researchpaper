From natural philosophy, and the study of the stars, I had a great deal of interest, and a comprehensive sense of the term, became nearly my sole occupation. I read those books which so many inquirers have written on these subjects. I attended the lectures at university and cultivated the acquaintance of science of the university, combined, it is true, with a repulsive physiognomy and manners, but not on account of less value. In a thousand ways he smoothed me the path of knowledge and made me the application of my inquiries at first fluctuating and uncertain; it gained strength as I proceeded and soon became so ardent and eager that the stars often disappeared from the light of the morning. my progress in the laboratory. As applied so closely, it may be easily conceived that my students were rapid. my ardor and my proficiency that of the masters. Professor Krempe often asked, with a smile, how Cornelius Agruppa went on, whilst M. Waldau expressed the most heart-felt appreciation of this manner, during which I paid no visit to Geneva, but was engaged, heartily, in my progress.
