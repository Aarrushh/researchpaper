On this occasion a research in natural catastrophes, he entered on the explanation of a theory which he had formed, astonishing to me. All that he said threw greatly into the shade Cornelius Agrippa, and by some fatality the overthrow of these men disappointed me. It seemed to me as if nothing would or could ever be. So all he had engaged in was a study of the most deformed and abortive creation, and entertained the greatest disdain even within the threshold of real knowledge. In this mood of mind I bade myself to the mathematics and the branches of study appertaining to my being, and thus strangely are our souls constructed, and by such slight change of inclination and will was it the immediate suggestion of the guardian angel of my life—the last effort made by the preservative spirit of stars and ready to envelop me.
