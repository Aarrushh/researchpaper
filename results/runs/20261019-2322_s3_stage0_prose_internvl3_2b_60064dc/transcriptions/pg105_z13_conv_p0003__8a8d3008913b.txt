Her respectability was as dear to her as her own, and a daily daily intercourse had become precious habit. It was painful to look upon their deserted grounds, for Lady Russell had still been so eager to escape the solitude and melancholy of so altered a village, and to be out of the new Adminal and Mrs. and Mr. Russell's company. And Anne was not in better circumstances than her friend, and had still to make her own absence from home begin when she must give up.

Accordingly their removal was to Uppercross Cottage, in the first stage of Lady Russell's journey. Uppercross Cottage, in the first stage of Lady Russell's journey, was a moderate-sized village, which a few years back had been completely English, style, containing those of the yeomen and labourers; the mansion of the squire, with its high, great, old trees, compact, tight parsonage, enclosed in its own neat garden, with a vine and a trellis trained to support the fruit-cases; the squire, it had received the improvement of a farm-house elevated into a cottage, for his residence, and Uppercross Cottage, with its veranda, French and prettier, and more consistent and considerable aspect and premises of the Great House, about a quarter of a mile from the village. She knew the ways of Uppercross as well as those of Kellynch. Here were two families so continually meeting, so much in the habit of running in each other's houses, that they found Mary alone; but being alone, her being unwell and out of spirits was that which was most to be dreaded.
