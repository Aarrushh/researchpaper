And Anne walked up at the same time, in a sort of drowsy irresolution, to the library, where she was to spend the free time. Her fixed eyes cast in her brother's spirits that hour. Lady Russell found her at break-up of the family assembly. Their respectability was a duty to her as to her son, and a daily rehearsal of it became necessary to keep it. It was perilous to look upon their deserved grounds, and still worse to anticipate the new hands who were to fall in; and to escape the solicitudes and the restraints of the world, she had been accustomed to shut herself up in her own room, and to read, or to sit in her own little garden, where she had a seat under the old oak, which she must give up soon. After her removal was made, and Anne was set down in Uppercross House, in the first stage of Lady Russell's journey, Uppercross was a moderate-sized village, with a few new houses built recently in the dull gray style, containing only two houses superior in appearance to those of the old inhabitants. The house of the Miss Bateses was one of these, and the house of the Miss Lysters another.
