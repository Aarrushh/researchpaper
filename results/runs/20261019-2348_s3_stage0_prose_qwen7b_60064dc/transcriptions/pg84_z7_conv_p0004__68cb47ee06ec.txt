Chance or rather the evil influence, the Angel of Destruction, which assented one-pointed way over me from the moment I turned my reluctant steps from my father's door-led me first to M. Krempe, professor of natural philosophy. He was an uncouth man, but deeply imbued in the secrets of his science. He asked me several questions concerning my progress in the different branches of science upon which I had been engaged. I was not able to answer them satisfactorily, for I had not read the works of the most celebrated of the authors I had studied. The professor stared. "Have you," he said, "really spent your time in studying such nonsense?" I replied in the affirmative. "Every minute," continued M. Krempe with warmth, "every instant that you have wasted on those books is utterly and entirely lost. You have burrowed yourself into a hole, and you will never be able to get out of it. I have a great deal of work to do, and I have no time to waste in order to inform you that these fancies which you so greatly imbited are a thousand years old and as musty as they are ancient!
