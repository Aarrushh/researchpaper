of people; friendly and hospitable, not much
educated, and not at all elegant. Their children
had more modern minds and manners. There was
a numerous family; but the only two grown up,
excepting Charles, were Henrietta and Louisa,
young ladies of nineteen and twenty, who had
brought from school at Exeter all the usual stock
of accomplishments, and were now like
thousands of other young ladies, living to be
fashionable, happy, and merry. Their dress had
