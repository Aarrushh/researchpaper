And on Mrs Musgrove's side, it was, "I make a rule of never interfering in any of my daughter-in-law's concerns, for I know it would not do; but I shall tell you, Miss Anne, because you may be able to set things to rights, that I have no very good opinion of Mrs Charles's nursery-maid: I hear my own declaration of her; she is always upon the gad; and from my knowledge, I can make you know, she is such a fine-dressing lady, that she is enough to ruin any servants but she comes near her. Mrs Charles quite swears by her, I know; just this hint; but I may be you may be so watchful; because, if you see anything amiss, you need not be afraid of mentioning it."
