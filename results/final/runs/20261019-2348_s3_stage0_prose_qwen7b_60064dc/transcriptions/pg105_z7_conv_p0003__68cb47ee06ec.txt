Lady Russell felt it but the affair of the family secondarily. Their respectability was as close to her as her own, and a daily intercourse had become precious by habit. It was painful to look upon their deserted grounds, and still worse to anticipate the new hands they were to fall into; and to escape the solitude and the melancholy of so altered a village, and be out of the way when Admiral and Mrs Croft were expected, was a great relief. The house was now in a state of transition. The great house, which had been built by her father, had been made over, and Anne was set down at Uppercross Cottage, in the first stage of Lady Russell's journey. Uppercross was a moderate-sized village, which a few years back had been completely in the old English style, containing only two houses superior in appearance to those of the younger sort. The great house had been built by the father of the present owner, and was a large, square, white building, with a long, low, compact, tight parsonage, enclosed in its own neat garden, with a view and a pear-tree trained round its casements: but upon the marriage of the young 'squire, it had received the improvement of a farm-house elevated into a cottage, for his residence, and Uppercross Cottage, with its verandah and its garden, had been built for the lady. The great house was now in the hands of a new tenant, and the cottage was let to the steward and premises of the Great House, about a quarter of a mile farther on. Here Anne had often been staying.
