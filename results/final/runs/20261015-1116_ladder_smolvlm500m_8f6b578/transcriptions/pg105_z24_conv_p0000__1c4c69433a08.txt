 Sir Walter Elliot, of Kelynhill Hall, in Somersetshire, was a man who, for his own amusement, never took up any book. But the Baronetage he found occupation for an idle hour, and consolation in a distressed sense. He found his faculties roused in admiration and respect, by contemplating the limited remanent of the earliest patents; and every new creation of the last century, and there, if every other leaf were powerless, he could read his own history with an interest never failed.
