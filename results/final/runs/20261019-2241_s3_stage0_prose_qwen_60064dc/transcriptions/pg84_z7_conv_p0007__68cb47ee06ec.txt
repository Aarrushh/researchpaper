I explained to the men of his various sciences, instructed me to let it be taught to preserve and promote the use of the time when I should have advanced far enough in the science to set their mechanics. He also gave me the list of books which I had requested, and I took my leave. This ended a day memorable to me; I decided my future destiny. Chapter 4 From this natural philosophy, and particularly chemistry, in the most comprehensive sense of the term, became nearly my sole occupation. I read with ardour these works, so full of genius, and I was so much struck with the beauty of the subject, that I was almost tempted to abandon the other sciences, and devote myself entirely to science of the university, and I found even in Krieml a great deal of sound sense and real information, combined, it is true, with a repulsive physiognomy and manners, but not to that account the slight value in. I was a true friend. His gentleness was never tinged by depravity, and his heart was as pure as his mind. I was not a little surprised at the manner in which he treated me, and I was not a little flattered. He smoothened for the most part knowledge and made the most abstruse inquiries clear and facile to me.
