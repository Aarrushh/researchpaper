It was not smothered by the shock, but entirely reduced to the thin remains of wood. I never behold anything so utterly destroyed. Before it was not unaccustomed with the more obvious laws of electricity. On this occasion a man of great research in natural philosophy was with, and excited by this catastrophe, he entered on the explanation of a theory which he had formed on the subject of electricity and galvanism, which was at once new and astonishing to me. All that he said threw greatly into the shade Agrippa, Albertus, Magnus, and Paracelsus, the lords of my imagination; but by some fatality the overture of his discourse was so full of the most beautiful and interesting things, that I could not but be delighted with it, and would ever be delighted. All that had so long engaged my attention suddenly grew despicable. By one of those caprices of the mind which we are perhaps most subject to in early youth, I at once gave up my former occupations, set down natural history and all its progeny as a deformed and abortive creation, and entertained the greatest disdain for a would-be science which could never even step within the threshold of real knowledge. In this state of mind I was not long in coming to the conclusion that the science of electricity was not that which science as being built upon secure foundations, and so worthy of my consideration. This strangely are our souls constructed, and by such slight ligaments are we bound to prosperity or ruin. When I look back, it seems to me as if this almost miraculous change of inclination and will was the immediate suggestion of the guardian angel of my life the last effort made by the spirit of preservation to avert the storm that was even then hanging in the starry sky.
