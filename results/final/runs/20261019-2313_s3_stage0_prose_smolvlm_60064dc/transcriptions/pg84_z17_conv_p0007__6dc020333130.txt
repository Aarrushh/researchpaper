 When she heard that the life of her favourite was menaced, she menaced, too, her mother. She attended her sickbed; her watchful attentions triumphed over the malignancy of the distemper-Elizabeth was saved, but the consequences of this imprudence were fatal to her preserver. On the third day she sickened; her fever was accompanied by most alarming symptoms, and the looks of her medical attendants prognosticated the worst event. On her deathbed the fortitude and benignity of this best of women, herself, she joined the hands of Elizabeth and herself, "My children, my firmest hopes of future happiness were placed on the prospect of your union. This will now be fulfilled."
