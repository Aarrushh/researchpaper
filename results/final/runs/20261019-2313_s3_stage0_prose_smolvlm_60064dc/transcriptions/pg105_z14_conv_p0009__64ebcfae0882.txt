 No to Bath till Lady Russell took her, and that all the intervening time between Upper Bishopsgate Cottage and Kellynch Lodge. So far all was perfectly right; but Lady Russell was almost startled by the wrong of her part of the Kellynch plan, when it burst on her in Mrs Clay's being engaged to Bath with Sir Walter, and in the latter all the business before her. Lady Russell was extremely sorry that a measure should have been so much to her, and she felt the imprudence of her intrusion, and the awkwardness of her acquaintance, and a knowledge of the quiet devotion, and the serious affection, and the more serious devotion of her father's character, that results from his most serious relations to his family from the intimacy which she had so soon entered into.
